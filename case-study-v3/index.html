The starting point

Payman already had a clear headline, a concrete payment example and detailed safeguards. I used those existing ingredients as the foundation for the redesign.

One payment, four steps

I kept the original headline and brought a request, policy check, approval and audit log into a simple sequence near the top. My intention was to introduce both what the agent does and how the bank stays in control.

A payment, step by step

The hero’s four cards return as a working sequence: the customer asks, the policy check runs, a person approves with a code, and the payment is logged. A bank can follow one payment from start to finish before it books a demo.

Design language: paperwork

I kept Payman’s teal, yellow and mauve, and developed a paperwork-inspired language: serif type, hairline rules, small labels and document-like cards.

Making control visible

I gave the bank’s rules, audit trail and kill switch a prominent section, then organized the detailed security information into three tabs. Each tab pairs a plain-language summary with technical details.

A small system for the site

I brought the direction together through a consistent type scale, color palette and reusable elements, including buttons, cards and tabs. These choices helped the prototype feel cohesive and provide a starting point for expanding the site.

A working prototype

I brought the redesign to life as a responsive website, using AI tools to help explore and build the experience. This was a self-initiated project; the outcome is the design and working prototype.
