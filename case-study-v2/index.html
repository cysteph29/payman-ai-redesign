Payman today

Payman puts AI agents inside banks’ and credit unions’ apps that carry out real payments and transfers, under the bank’s own rules. Its site already had the right material: proof from banks, a working product demo and real safeguards. The order kept the strongest parts low on the page.

One payment, four clear steps

The original site already showed a customer requesting vendor payments and approving them with a code, and described policy and an audit trail further down the page. I brought those pieces into one sequence and placed it at the top of the redesign, to help a bank understand what the agent does and where the bank retains control.

Design language: paperwork

Banks run on well-kept paperwork, so the page borrows its materials: ruled stock, hatched paper, filed cards and form labels. It gives an AI product the calm, exact feel of a bank document, in the brand’s own colors.

A payment, step by step

The hero’s four cards return as a working sequence: the customer asks, the policy check runs, a person approves with a code, and the payment is logged. A bank can follow one payment from start to finish before it books a demo.

Control and security, in the same language

The bank’s controls get their own section straight after the opening. Security is grouped into three tabs, each led by a plain-language promise with its specs beside it, so an executive gets the point and a compliance team gets the detail.

A small system for a marketing site

It’s a landing page, not a product, so the system stays small: a type scale, colors with set jobs, and the few elements every section is built from. Defined once, they keep new pages on brand and give developers one clear spec.

Built with AI, decided by hand

I used AI tools to explore and build quickly, which made it possible to design and ship a working, responsive site on my own. The direction and the judgment calls were mine.
